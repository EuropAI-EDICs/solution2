          4.1 Success metrics
          Describe the pilot’s success metrics.
          Note that, EUROPAI project-level ambitions: >=20% ef ciency gain, >=15% reduction in administrative burden and >=10% improvement in citizen
          satisfaction over baseline. Each pilot should assess the applicability of these ambitions and translate them into relevant pilot-speci c targets. These
           gures do not automatically apply to every KPI. Each KPI must de ne a baseline, target, minimum acceptance, measurement method, evidence
          source, timing and owner:
          Metric:      What exactly will be measured, and in what unit or scale? (some example themes are given in the table below)
          Baseline:      What is the current value, and what is the reference point for comparison?
          Target:      What result is expected by the end of the pilot?
          Minimum acceptance: What is the lowest result that would still be considered acceptable?
          Method:        How will the KPI be measured or calculated? (some example methods are given in the table below)
          Evidence:        What data source, document, log, survey, test result or other evidence will substantiate the measurement?
          Timing:        When, and how often, will the measurement be taken?
          Owner:        Who is responsible for collecting, validating and reporting the KPI?
