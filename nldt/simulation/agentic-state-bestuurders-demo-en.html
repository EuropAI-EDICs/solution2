How AI assistants are reshaping the work of government — and how our toolbox does it verifiably: AI proposes, the pipeline decides, and a human keeps the final button. Working today, not on paper.

The frame

The whitepaper The Agentic State (Ilves et al., 2025) maps agentic AI across twelve functional layers with an L0–L5 autonomy scale.

The approach

We deliberately operate at L3: agentic workflows in a bounded domain, with gates and a learning trail as the safety net.

Today's proof

The human–agent boundary is live: the assistant pauses, a human decides with a mandatory justification, and the decision feeds the learning loop.

1 · Why now

Agentic AI is the biggest disruption
to government since the modern state

Earlier automation repeated existing processes. Agents pursue outcomes: they plan, reason and act within boundaries — arriving in weeks rather than decades. The whitepaper is explicit: lead the transformation or be transformed by it.

The window is narrow

ChatGPT reached 100 million users in two months; the telephone took 75 years. Proactive choices must be made now.

The pressure is national

Housing, climate adaptation, energy transition and water demand faster, better-substantiated spatial decisions — with underpinnings that hold up.

Our answer

Don't wait for L5. Build at the level that is responsible for this domain — and prove every step.

Our position: the toolbox is no longer an experiment but a reference implementation — anchored in the national Zicht op Netherlands vision, the European DTAS vision and the EDIC reference architecture.

Source: Ilves et al. (2025), ch. 1 · nldt/bestuurlijke-samenvatting.md.

3 · Our profile

L3 in a bounded domain — exactly right, not "not there yet"

"A reliable Level 3 system may beat an unpredictable Level 4 in sensitive domains." — The Agentic State (Ilves et al., 2025): a higher autonomy level is not automatically better.

Where we are strong

Workflows (L3): the full chain from question to substantiated dossier, with validation gates. Governance, data and stack (L2–L3): provenance per figure, authoritative sources, swappable models — done right from day one.

Where the paper still wins

Compliance is periodic, not yet continuous. Service UX waits for questions, is not yet proactive. Security must be explicitly designed for autonomous systems. That is the roadmap, not a shame.

Why this matters to the board: a reliable L3 system yields predictable, accountable decisions. An unpredictable L4 delivers speed without durability — precisely what objection and judicial review do not survive.

Source: gap analysis §4 reading guide · slides Agentic AI in Spatial Planning.

4 · The method — Sitra

Redesign before technology: function-first, five gates, learning trail

The Finnish research (Sitra, 2026) provides the method we follow. One shared function — preparing a spatial assessment — across municipalities, provinces and water boards. The six PoCs (Utrecht, Breda, Eindhoven, Rijnland, crosstrack, MiniGIM) are instances of that single function.

gate 1

Public outcome

Baseline: what does it cost now, which work disappears?

gate 2

Shared meaning

Authoritative sources — cited or silent.

gate 3

Legal & human boundary

What may the agent do, when does it escalate? Live today.

gate 4

Replaceable architecture

Models and vendors are swappable.

gate 5

Evidence & stop conditions

Every run replayable; stopping is possible, with evidence.

Technology is not the starting point: redesign work and data first, systems last. The greatest gain may be that a step disappears — not that it speeds up.

Source: Sitra working paper (2026) · docs/AGENTIC_STATE_PLAN.md §1 (B1–B14) and §5 phasing.

5 · Gate 3, live today

The human–agent boundary works — not on paper

Today this chain completed the full cycle: the assistant wanted to run the zoning-plan conversion (Eindhoven, bp2op) and had to pause for a human decision. Here is what happened:

step 1 · agent

Assistant proposes

The agent plans the conversion and invokes the transform — per MC-6: nothing is linked by AI; the jurist decides.

step 2 · gate

Schema gate catches it

Format and provenance are checked; the state-changing action may not simply pass.

step 3 · the human

Dashboard: decision

The card shows tool + arguments. Approve or reject — always with a mandatory justification. No clock that ever auto-approves.

step 4 · resume

The run continues safely

On approval the transform runs; on rejection it is skipped and the dossier stays with the jurist (V4 pending).

step 5 · learn

Learning trail

The decision becomes a trail carrying your justification — the input for the learning report across three levels.

Why this matters: this is gate 3 as a mechanism. Every state-changing AI action in this domain now has a demonstrable human button.

Source: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-hitl-mens-agent-grens-design.md · execution status in the implementation plan (16 commits, final review approved).

6 · The other side

What happens when you click "reject"

Rejecting is not a malfunction but a first-class decision — and it is visible everywhere:

The action stays out

The transform is not executed. The assistant sees the rejection verdict and continues within its boundaries.

The dossier stays with the jurist

V4 remains pending-by-design: the substantive jurist check never moves to the dashboard button.

It learns

Journal, run log and learning trail all carry the rejection with your justification. Rejections are the richest input for the learning report.

No silent approval

There is no timer that auto-approves an open decision. An open question stays open until a human decides.

No machine masquerade

The dev flag for automatic approval (tests) marks itself as machine-made in the register — never visible as a human decision.

No lone decider

Every decision carries operator, justification and timestamp. Traceable at objection, review and audit.

Source: HITL spec §3–§4 · nldt/07-trust-and-governance.md ("no autonomous high-risk or final legal decision").

8 · Example — Province of Utrecht

Where can wind, solar and forest go — with every rule cited

The province's question: within which zone may the regional regulation allow what? The answer is an opportunity map in which every rule is cited — instrument, article, version, verbatim quote and URL. 24 norm cards per track; the 13 deliberately ambiguous open rules are never guessed and always go to a human.

The what-if sweep

The agent proposes policy variants; the engine computes them deterministically:

NNN-clause-2 exception → +322.5 km² (+37.5%) wind
quiet area as hard exclusion → up to −61%
Natura 2000 buffer 500 m (hypothetical) → −52.2%
solar: Natura carve-out at face value → +0.011 km² (symbolic)

What the agent adds

The agent finds exactly the variants a policy officer would consider — including the unexpected ones. In the canonical run 10 of 10 scenario proposals were accepted, every rule id real (local model, temperature 0).

Who does what

The engine produces the number, the critic guards the truth about it (if the agent hallucinates a rule id, it lands in the rejection ledger), the human weighs the trade-off.

Why this matters to the board: the trade-off dialogue ("wind here, but then we hit the quiet area") moves from weeks of desk research to one repeatable, fully cited session — with the same numbers for council, province and objector.

Source: canonical run 20260830T113234Z-wind (Province of Utrecht) · poc/ · slides Agentic AI in Spatial Planning, PoC-1.

9 · Deliberately not

We also say no — and record why

Three layers score L0 with us: not forgotten, but deliberately out of scope. Recording that is itself part of the approach — a framework that only scores what flatters is an advert, not an analysis.

Layer 5 · Crisis response

The toolbox's domain is spatial planning. Machine-speed crisis coordination belongs to a different mandate.

Layer 6 · Procurement & layer 11 · Finance

The channel exists (EU marketplace, dataspace offers); autonomous negotiation is not intended. Funding the learning period is a process question for you.

Prescriptive AI, autonomous interventions

No AI that rules on legal outcomes or intervenes in physical reality. The recommendation stays with the human — recorded in the EDIC conformity assessment.

And about the open flanks: what we cannot do yet is documented in the gap analysis — continuous supervision (layer 4), proactive service delivery (layer 1) and agent-specific security (layer 10) are the named next gaps.

Source: gap analysis §2–§4 (layers 5, 6, 11) · executive summary ("deliberately not").

10 · Ask of the board

What we ask

1 · Endorse the approach

"One calculation core, multiple access points, with a demonstrable human–agent boundary" as the guiding principle for AI in spatial planning — alongside the already-endorsed ZoN digital-twin agenda.

2 · Mandate for the first function

Scale the shared function "preparing a spatial assessment" to at least three organisational contexts (Utrecht, Breda, Eindhoven, Rijnland are ready) — in line with the five gates.

3 · Name the function steward

One driver with a bounded mandate, a decision-point calendar and stop conditions: continue, adjust, pause or stop — always on the basis of evidence.

4 · Take the learning trail seriously

Every signal becomes a trail; the learning report is a phase exit, not an option. From now on, the human–agent boundary supplies that report with decisions.

Small start, hard edge: the first two phases take days to a few sprints. The stop conditions are no formality — pausing is an outcome the framework can handle.
